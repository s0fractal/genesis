# Task 0124: Era 1380 — Forensic Event Sink

## Status: COMPLETED | Priority: HIGH

## Context
Multiple Eras now emit "events" with stable per-event hashes:
Era 1370's `ConvergenceAlarmEvent`, Era 1290's quorum verdicts,
Era 1200's partition alarms. None had unified durable storage.
Era 1380 introduces an append-only chain-anchored event log
generic enough for all of them.

## Objective
1. `ForensicEventSink` class with bounded capacity + FIFO eviction.
2. Per-entry `chain_hash` linking to predecessor (tamper detection).
3. `verifyChain()` returning the first broken sequence or null.
4. `eventChainAnchor()` for cross-relay anchoring (FNV-1a over
   sorted event_hash set, mirrors Era 1310).
5. `diffEventSinks` set-difference primitive for future wire sync.

## Design Notes
- **Why a chain hash AND an event_hash?** They mean different
  things. `event_hash` identifies the *content* of the event
  (computed by the originating Era — Era 1370 gives us alarm
  hashes, Era 1290 gives us verdict digests). `chain_hash` orders
  the *log itself* — it's what makes "the third event in this
  log was X, with Y as its predecessor" tamper-evident. They
  serve perpendicular concerns.
- **Why FIFO eviction instead of priority/LRU?** Forensic
  semantics: events are time-ordered facts about what happened.
  Recent events are typically more useful for postmortem than
  ancient ones, and operators expect "the log forgets oldest
  first" without surprising reorderings. LRU would create
  weird gaps; priority would invite policy debates this layer
  shouldn't host.
- **Why does sequence persist across eviction?** Two reasons.
  First, surviving entries' `prev_chain_hash` chain only verifies
  if their own sequence numbers don't shift. Second, an operator
  saying "I lost event seq #1247" expects that number to be
  globally meaningful within the lifetime of this sink, not
  ephemeral and reset.
- **Why is the payload `unknown`?** The sink shouldn't introspect.
  Different events have different shapes (alarm vs verdict vs
  partition); making the sink generic over them avoids coupling
  Era 1380 to every upstream Era's payload contract. The
  `event_hash` is the cross-Era contract; everything else is
  consumer-specific.
- **Why no built-in serialization?** Persistence to disk/wire is
  Era 1390's job (sync over wire) and outside this Era's scope.
  Keeping the sink in-memory + pure data structure makes its
  invariants (chain integrity, eviction semantics) testable
  without I/O surface area.

## Next Era Trigger
**Era 1390: Event Sink Sync over Wire.** Era 1380 stores events
locally but offers no protocol for cross-relay reconciliation.
Era 1390 will apply the same set-difference idiom Era 1310 used
for archives: a peer broadcasts its `eventChainAnchor` + sorted
event_hash list; receiver responds with an `EventDelta` containing
the entries the broadcaster lacks; chunked over SporeFrame wire
via Era 1320 mechanics. Collision rejection (same event_hash
with different payload bytes → corruption signal) preserves the
hash-as-content-address invariant the rest of the protocol relies
on.
