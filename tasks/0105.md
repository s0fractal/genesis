# Task 0105: Era 1190 — Resilience Snapshot Export

## Status: COMPLETED | Priority: HIGH

## Context
Era 1180 measures `redundancy_rate` per-relay. Era 1190 turns that
measurement into a wire format so peer relays can broadcast their
own statistics, compare them, and detect partitions when their
views of the mesh's health diverge.

## Objective
A 32-byte fixed-width binary report carrying every field from a
relay's `ConvergenceDetector.stats()` plus a Q16 fixed-point
representation of the headline `redundancy_rate`. The report is
CRC-protected, magic-tagged ("RS" = `0x5253`), version-tagged
(currently v1), and CRC-validated on receipt — drop on any drift.

## Design Notes
- **Why Q16 fixed-point on the wire?** Floating-point would force
  every conforming implementation (browser JS, Rust, ZK guest,
  embedded firmware) to agree on IEEE-754 details that aren't
  guaranteed across compilers. Integer Q16 is exact in every
  implementation. The Q16 step (1/65536 ≈ 0.0015%) is finer than
  any operationally meaningful redundancy difference.
- **Why FNV-1a as the CRC again?** Same reason as `spore_frame.rs`:
  single source of FNV-1a in the kernel, no extra constant tables,
  and frame integrity is the only thing this CRC protects (the
  upper layers' anchors handle semantic collisions).
- **Magic = "RS" (0x5253)** — distinct from `spore_frame`'s
  "OF" so a relay seeing a mixed binary stream can tell snapshot
  frames from senate frames at the byte level.
- **Two relays disagreeing by ≥10% redundancy is alarming because**
  both are observing the same physical mesh; if their measurements
  diverge that much, either (a) the network has split into
  partitions with different reliability profiles, or (b) one relay
  is compromised. Either way, the operator wants to know.
