# Task 0123: Era 1370 — Convergence-Triggered Auto Sync

## Status: COMPLETED | Priority: HIGH

## Context
Era 1350 raises an `alarm` when convergence drops below a
threshold. Era 1340's coordinator selects sync peers by schedule.
Era 1360 aggregates peer digest lists into a network-known set.
Until now these layers were independent — the alarm was visible
but nothing reacted to it. Era 1370 closes the reactive loop:
when alarm fires, immediately sync with the peer that adds the
most missing digests, bypassing per-peer cooldown.

## Objective
1. `rankPeersByNovelty(agg, local_digests, now_ms)` — score peers
   by set-difference cardinality (NEW digests they'd contribute).
2. `selectMostInformativePeer` — top-1 picker, null when nothing
   to gain.
3. `selectAlarmOverrideOrder(coord, agg, local, now_ms, max)` —
   priority list bypassing Era 1330's cooldown but respecting
   cold-peer exclusion.
4. `convergenceAlarmEvent` — forensic event payload with
   FNV-1a hash anchor for cross-relay corroboration.

## Design Notes
- **Why set-difference cardinality instead of FNV-overlap or
  Jaccard?** At realistic fleet scales (hundreds to low thousands
  of digests per peer), straight set-difference is O(N) with tiny
  constants and gives us exactly the metric that matters: "how
  many MORE digests would I have if I synced from this peer?".
  Jaccard would tell us *similarity*; we want *expected gain*.
- **Why exclude cold peers from alarm override?** Cooldown bypass
  exists because schedule dampening can be too conservative under
  poor convergence. But a peer that has failed `failure_giveup_count`
  times in a row is probably not going to start working just
  because we ask harder. The cold gate is a different signal —
  partner is broken, not slow — and convergence alarm doesn't
  override broken-partner detection.
- **Why exclude peers unknown to the coordinator?** The aggregator
  may have observations from peers the coordinator hasn't been
  formally introduced to (e.g. arrived via a multi-hop forwarder).
  Initiating a sync to such a peer would skip the coordinator's
  contract — no peer state to track success/failure, no scheduling
  hook. The override defers peer-discovery to the appropriate
  Era (1130 + 1140 reputation routing) and only acts on tracked
  peers.
- **Why FNV-1a hash for the alarm event?** Same primitive as the
  rest of the protocol — Eras 1190 through 1360 anchor everything
  in FNV-1a. Cross-relay event corroboration ("we both saw this
  alarm at the same moment with the same network view") becomes
  a one-line hash comparison. New cryptographic primitives would
  break the determinism stack.
- **Why is the alarm event pure data, not auto-routed to a log?**
  Different deployments have different durability needs. A spore
  with no flash storage just emits the event over the wire; an
  archivist node persists every event for legal hold. Era 1380
  will provide the durable log; Era 1370 produces the payload
  whose semantics are stable across both backends.

## Next Era Trigger
**Era 1380: Forensic Event Sink.** Era 1370 produces
`ConvergenceAlarmEvent` with a stable hash, but routing it into
durable storage is left to the caller. Era 1380 will introduce
an append-only event log with FNV-1a chain-of-custody (each
event's hash includes its predecessor's hash, like a hash chain),
bounded eviction, and a cross-relay sync protocol modeled on
Era 1310's archive delta exchange. The same primitives that
guarantee archive convergence will guarantee event-log convergence
across the mesh.
