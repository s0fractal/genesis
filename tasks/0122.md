# Task 0122: Era 1360 — Network Digest Aggregation

## Status: COMPLETED | Priority: HIGH

## Context
Era 1350 produces a convergence health signal from `local_digests`
and `network_digests`. The first argument is straightforward (the
local archive's digest list); the second was left for the caller
to synthesize. Era 1360 closes that gap with
`NetworkDigestAggregator` — a TTL-bounded per-peer observation
store driven by the `ArchiveDigestList` shape Era 1310 already
defines.

## Objective
Provide a stateful aggregator that:
1. Records per-peer `ArchiveDigestList` observations with timestamps.
2. Returns the deduplicated sorted union of all currently-fresh peer
   digest sets via `networkDigests(now_ms)`.
3. Plumbs that union directly into Era 1350 via `convergenceSignal`.
4. Exposes operator-friendly telemetry.

## Design Notes
- **Why TTL eviction on read instead of in a separate "tick"?**
  The aggregator has no internal clock; eviction needs `now_ms`.
  Doing it on access keeps the API surface minimal and ensures
  every read sees a freshly-pruned state. Cost is negligible
  (one map walk per read; aggregator size is bounded by peer
  count, typically tens to low hundreds).
- **Why store `broadcast_at_ms` separately from `observed_at_ms`?**
  They mean different things. `observed_at_ms` is "when we heard
  about this digest list" (used for TTL); `broadcast_at_ms` is
  "when the peer claims to have built this list" (useful for
  detecting clock skew or stale rebroadcasts). Kept separate so
  future Eras can reason about either.
- **Why defensive sort on input?** `ArchiveDigestList` from Era
  1310's `buildDigestList` is sorted, but we accept lists from
  the wire (chunked, reassembled, possibly from older
  implementations). Storing canonically sorted ensures
  `networkDigests` output is stable regardless of caller's
  serialization discipline.
- **Why doesn't a peer-shrink trigger an alarm here?** Some
  legitimate causes: archive rotation, retention policy,
  recovery from corruption. Detecting *suspicious* shrinks
  belongs in Era 1220's investigation convergence (which already
  tracks behavior anomalies); the aggregator is pure
  observation. Keeping these concerns separate avoids tangling
  network-coverage measurement with adversarial behavior
  detection.
- **Why no "expected peer set" enforcement?** A relay shouldn't
  refuse to update its convergence view just because a peer it
  doesn't expect started broadcasting. The mesh is open by
  construction — surprises are routine. Era 1220 handles
  evaluating whether new peers are trustworthy; the aggregator
  just measures coverage of whoever showed up.
